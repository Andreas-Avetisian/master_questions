# Name and explain the four main sources for research funding from the lecture. What are their characteristics?

The lecture distinguishes four main ways research work is financed: **basic/internal budgets**, **teaching**, **contracted research**, and **funded research / research grants**.

## 1. Basic budgets / internal budget

This is institutional money available inside a university or research organization.

Typical examples:

- universities
- some research organizations such as AIT

Characteristics:

- Often comes from state or government budgets.
- Can be a flat rate or paid positions.
- Often supports long-term or lifetime positions.
- Usually has fixed rules and wages.
- Typically no project-style reporting is required.
- It is **not considered external research funding** in the strict sense.
- It can be used to **leverage** additional external funding.

Important limitation from the lecture: this is not generally available in the same way for Universities of Applied Sciences.

## 2. Teaching

Teaching can indirectly finance research time, especially at Universities of Applied Sciences.

Characteristics:

- The institution receives payment from the government per student place.
- The money is primarily tied to teaching, not to research.
- Usage depends on UAS-specific internal rules.
- No project-style reporting is required.
- There are usually no fixed research-wage rules in the same sense as grants.
- It is **not research funding per se**, but it can free or allocate lecturer time for research.

The lecture notes that FHSTP has a comparatively large amount of lecturer time assigned to research, which makes teaching-related financing relevant in practice.

## 3. Contracted research

Contracted research means a customer directly pays an institution or research group for a research or development task.

Typical actors:

- universities
- universities of applied sciences
- research centers
- COMET centers
- industrial research labs
- companies

Characteristics:

- Works like a normal purchase order in the private sector.
- Usually business-to-business contracts and rates are freely negotiated.
- There is no funding agency competition in the same sense as grants.
- It is often **not funding**, but can be used as leverage for funding.
- Contractual liabilities are possible.
- Wages, deliverables, intellectual property, and deadlines depend on the contract.

This form is close to commissioned work: someone has a concrete need and pays for the work.

## 4. Funded research / research grants

Funded research is the classic external research-funding model.

Characteristics:

- It is a major funding instrument for universities, UAS, and research centers.
- Money is typically awarded as a grant.
- Applications are often competitive.
- There can be local, national, and international funding agencies.
- Examples from the lecture: **GFF**, **WWTF**, **FFG**, **FWF**, **CDG**, **EU / Horizon Europe**, **ERASMUS+**.
- Grants usually have a short- to mid-term project perspective.
- There is a time gap between proposal writing, approval, and actual payment.
- Often only part of the costs is refunded.
- Upfront payment capability is important because costs may be reimbursed later.
- Proposals can be single-stage or multi-stage and may involve hearings.

In this model, success depends strongly on writing good proposals and choosing the correct funding scheme.

## Short summary

The four sources are: **internal/basic budgets** (institutional baseline money, often state-funded, little reporting), **teaching** (not research funding itself but can finance research time), **contracted research** (customer-paid B2B research with negotiated contracts), and **funded research/grants** (competitive external funding from agencies such as FFG, FWF or EU/Horizon Europe). The key difference is whether the money comes from inside the institution, from teaching allocation, from a customer contract, or from a competitive funding program.
